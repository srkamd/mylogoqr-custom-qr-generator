The practical guide to branded QR codes

MWritten by MyLogoQR Editorial Team — Last updated October 2026

A QR code is just a picture that stores text. When a phone camera reads it, the phone decides what to do with that text: open a link, start a WhatsApp chat, join a Wi-Fi network or save a contact. Understanding this one idea explains almost every design rule below, because anything that makes the picture harder to read makes the code harder to scan.

Static codes, and why they never expire

MyLogoQR creates static codes. Your link, number or password is written directly into the pattern, and nothing passes through our servers. That means a code you made years ago still works today as long as the destination exists. The trade-off is that the content cannot be edited later. If you print 500 flyers with a typo in the URL, the flyers must be reprinted, so always open the link yourself before exporting.

How much logo is too much?

Every QR code contains spare data called error correction. A logo hides part of the pattern, and the spare data fills the gap. Level Q can rebuild up to 25 percent of the code and level H up to 30 percent. In practice a logo covering about a fifth of the width scans reliably on most phones, while a logo above 30 percent starts to fail on older devices and in poor light.

Quick test: scan the preview with two different phones, once in daylight and once indoors, before you send anything to print.

Choosing colors that scan

Scanners look for a dark pattern on a lighter background. A navy or deep green pattern on white is as reliable as black. Problems appear when both colors have similar brightness, for example yellow on white, or when the code is inverted to light-on-dark, which some older apps cannot read. Gradients are safe if even the lightest part of the gradient stays clearly darker than the background.

Which QR type fits which job

  • Website link: menus, portfolios, event pages, app store listings.
  • WhatsApp: small shops and service businesses that take orders by chat. A pre-filled message saves the customer typing.
  • Wi-Fi: cafes, offices, rental properties. Guests skip typing long passwords.
  • Contact card: business cards, conference badges, email signatures.
  • Plain text: serial numbers, inventory labels and short notes that must work offline.

Print size and placement

As a rule, keep at least one centimetre of QR width for every ten centimetres of scanning distance. A code on a shop window read from two metres away should be about five centimetres wide at minimum. Leave a blank margin of four modules (the small squares) around the code and never place it on a curved or glossy surface that reflects light.

SVG or PNG?

SVG is a vector file that stays sharp at any size, so use it for signs, packaging and anything sent to a printer. PNG is a fixed-size picture that suits websites, slides and social posts. If you are unsure, download both.

Your privacy

The code is drawn inside your browser. Wi-Fi passwords and contact details never leave your device. Remember that anyone who scans a QR code can read what is inside it, so do not encode secrets you would not print on the poster itself.

Four steps to a code that scans every time

  1. Check your content

    A printed QR code cannot be edited, so test the link or number first.

  2. Keep strong contrast

    Use a dark pattern on a light background, even when using gradients.

  3. Size the logo carefully

    Stay near 20 to 25 percent and use error correction Q or H.

  4. Export the right format

    SVG for print and packaging, PNG for screens and social posts.

Error correction levels explained

QR codes include built-in redundancy, so they still scan when a logo covers part of the pattern. Higher levels recover more but make the code denser.

LevelRecoversBest for
LUp to 7%Plain text without a logo
MUp to 15%Simple codes, tiny logos
QUp to 25%Business cards and standard logos
HUp to 30%Large logos and outdoor prints

Frequently asked questions

Can I change a QR code after printing it?

No. These are static codes, so the data is part of the pattern. If your link or number changes, create a new code and reprint.

Do the codes expire or have a scan limit?

No. There is no redirect server, so nothing can expire and nothing needs a subscription.

What logo size is safe?

Stay at 20 to 25 percent, keep strong contrast and test with a real phone before printing.

Is my data stored anywhere?

No. Everything is generated in your browser. See the privacy policy.
