The Infamous 'Free Trial' QR Code Trap Explained

Many online QR code generators lure users with promises of 'Free QR Codes'. They generate a dynamic redirect link that routes through their private servers. After 14 days or 50 scans, they deactivate the redirect and display a paywall error page to your customers—unless you pay an expensive recurring monthly fee!

What Is a Static QR Code (And Why They Never Expire)

  • Zero Server Dependency: When a phone camera scans the code, it navigates directly to your website. Nothing passes through our servers.
  • Unlimited Lifetime Scans: A static QR code printed today will still work 10 years from now as long as your website exists. No monthly bills, no scan caps, and no expiration dates!

When Do You Actually Need a Dynamic QR Code?

Dynamic QR codes are only necessary if you require:

  1. Post-Print URL Changing: Changing where a printed billboard leads without physically replacing the billboard.
  2. Granular Scan Analytics: Tracking exact scan times, geographic cities, and mobile device types.

The real price of "free" dynamic QR codes

Dynamic QR services usually advertise a free tier, but the meter starts quickly: a handful of active codes, a scan cap per month, and a trial that ends with your printed codes held hostage behind a subscription. The business model depends on your print run — once a menu, sign or product line carries their code, cancelling feels impossible, so the monthly fee becomes permanent rent on your own marketing. Before choosing, price the whole horizon: a cafe paying even a modest monthly fee spends hundreds over the three years a set of table cards lasts, for codes that a free static generator produces identically. When dynamic pricing is actually worth paying

Dynamic codes earn their fee in narrow cases: a campaign where the destination must change weekly on already-printed material, or a business that genuinely acts on per-scan analytics. If neither describes you, the subscription buys risk, not features — including the worst risk of all, expiration. A lapsed dynamic plan can turn every printed code into a dead end overnight, while a static code fails only if your own destination page disappears. Read the full feature comparison in our dynamic vs static QR codes comparison, then choose with your print run, not the vendor's pricing page, in mind.

A five-year cost comparison, honestly done

Put real numbers on the choice. A dynamic plan at even ten dollars a month costs six hundred dollars over five years — per account, not per code — and the price buys a redirect you may never use. The static equivalent costs nothing in year one and nothing in year five, because there is no server in the middle to pay for. The features people imagine they lose are smaller than the brochures suggest: destination editing matters only when the destination itself was a mistake, and scan counts can be approximated with tagged links in your own analytics. What static cannot do is change after print — so the discipline it demands is simply checking the link before the print run, a habit worth more than the subscription. When a vendor's free trial ends and the dashboard offers to "save your codes" for a monthly fee, that message is the business model introducing itself.

The bottom line: you are not choosing between two kinds of picture — you are choosing between owning your codes outright and renting them back monthly. For fixed destinations, which is nearly everything in print, the free static code is the rational default, and the subscription only makes sense when changing destinations after printing is a genuine, recurring need that you will actually use every single month, not a just-in-case fear sold at checkout.

One last pricing trick deserves naming: the scan cap. Several "free" dynamic tiers quietly limit monthly scans, so a code that succeeds — a busy menu, a popular product — is the code most likely to be throttled or pushed into a paid tier mid-campaign. Success should never be the event that breaks your signage or starts a bill. A static code has no meter running: the thousandth scan costs exactly what the first one did, which is nothing, and that flat zero is precisely what printed material needs from the codes it carries into the world.

Frequently asked questions

Do static QR codes really never expire?

Yes. A static code contains its destination directly, with no middleman server, so it works for as long as the destination itself exists — no subscription can switch it off.

Why do dynamic QR codes stop working when you cancel?

Because the printed pattern points to the vendor's redirect server. When the plan lapses, the redirect is disabled and the code leads nowhere.

Can I get scan analytics with a static code?

Not from the code itself — that requires a redirect layer. You can still measure results with tagged links (UTM parameters) read by your own website analytics.
