How Much Logo Can a QR Code Have? (Safe Size Guide)

By Shahrukh Ahmad — Creator & Lead Systems Architect  |  Last updated: November 2026

Quick Answer

A QR code can safely hold a logo covering up to 30% of its surface area, but only if you use Level H (High) error correction. The practical sweet spot for branded QR codes is 20–25% — enough to show your logo clearly while keeping scan speed fast.

Why There Is a Logo Size Limit

QR codes store data in a grid of black and white squares called modules. When you place a logo in the center, it physically covers those modules — making that data unreadable. The QR standard (ISO/IEC 18004) includes a built-in recovery system called error correction that lets scanners reconstruct the hidden data.

There are four error correction levels:

Level Recovery Capacity Logo Safe?
L (Low) ~7% of modules ✗ No
M (Medium) ~15% of modules ⚠ Tiny logos only
Q (Quartile) ~25% of modules ⚠ Small logos
H (High) ~30% of modules ✓ Best for logos

The Formula: How to Calculate Logo Size

The logo size limit is based on area percentage, not width or height alone:

Logo Area % = (logo width × logo height) ÷ (QR width × QR height) × 100

Keep this number at or below 30% (ideally 20–25%)

Example Calculation

QR code: 300×300px  |  Logo: 75×75px

Logo area = 75 × 75 = 5,625 px²
QR area   = 300 × 300 = 90,000 px²
Percentage = 5,625 ÷ 90,000 × 100 = 6.25% ✓ Safe

Practical Size Recommendations by QR Code Size

QR Code Size Max Logo (30%) Recommended (22%)
200 × 200 px ~110 × 110 px ~94 × 94 px
500 × 500 px ~274 × 274 px ~235 × 235 px
1000 × 1000 px ~548 × 548 px ~470 × 470 px

Logo Shape Matters Too

Round logos are ideal because they minimize corner overlap with QR finder patterns (the three squares in the corners). Square logos work fine too. Avoid wide rectangular logos — they can clip the finder patterns and confuse scanners even at under 30% area.

  • Round or square: Best. Centered in the QR code.
  • Wide rectangle: Risky. Keep below 20% and add padding.
  • Tall rectangle: Can block the timing patterns (row 6 / column 6). Avoid.
  • Complex/busy logo: Add a white padding ring of at least 8px around it.

Logo Color & Contrast Rules

A logo that blends into the QR code background causes scan failures just as badly as an oversized one. Follow these contrast rules:

  • Logo background: white or very light (not transparent over dark QR modules)
  • Minimum contrast between QR modules and background: 4:1 ratio
  • Add a white padding ring (8–16px) around your logo to separate it from modules

Frequently Asked Questions

How much logo can a QR code have?

Up to 30% of its surface area with Level H error correction. The recommended sweet spot is 20–25% for the best balance of scan speed and logo visibility.

What error correction level do I need for a logo QR code?

Always Level H (High). Level H can reconstruct up to 30% of damaged or hidden data — which is exactly the room your logo needs. Never use Level L or M for logo QR codes.

Does a bigger logo hurt scan speed?

Yes. The larger the logo, the more data reconstruction the scanner must do. A 25% logo scans slightly slower than a 15% one. For public signage or high-traffic use, keep it under 25%.

Can I put a rectangular logo in a QR code?

Yes, but calculate area carefully. A wide rectangular logo can still exceed 30% even if it looks small. Crop or resize it and add white padding. Avoid placing it near the three finder-pattern squares in the corners.

My QR code with logo is not scanning — what should I check?

Check in this order: (1) logo over 30%? Shrink it. (2) Error correction set to H? Change it. (3) Low contrast? Add a white ring. (4) Printed too small? Minimum 2.5 cm / 1 inch. See our full guide: QR Code with Logo Not Scanning — 9 Fixes.
