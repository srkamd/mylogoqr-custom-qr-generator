How to Turn Your Logo into a Scannable QR Code (Step-by-Step)

MWritten by MyLogoQR Technical Team — October 29, 2026

Want to transform your company emblem or personal avatar into a scannable QR code? Here is the exact process to turn your logo into a high-performing QR code without technical glitches.

Step 1: Prepare Your Logo File

The quality of your central logo directly affects how professional the final barcode appears. Follow these graphic design tips before uploading:

  • Use Square Dimensions (1:1 Aspect Ratio): Center icons look best when cropped to a perfect square (e.g. 512x512 pixels).
  • Use a High-Contrast PNG with Transparent Background: A transparent PNG allows circular white badges to frame your logo naturally without awkward rectangular borders.
  • Simplify Complex Details: If your logo has small tagline text below it, remove the text and use only the core icon mark. Small micro-text inside a QR code becomes illegible when printed under 3 cm.

Step 2: Enter Your Destination Link or Contact Data

Choose what happens when someone scans your code. You can link to your homepage, an Instagram bio link, a direct WhatsApp chat greeting, or a digital business card.

Step 3: Upload and Scale the Logo Inside the Generator

Inside our generator tool, navigate to Section 5 (Logo) and click 'Upload logo'. Keep the Logo Size slider at 20% to 25%. This leaves over 75% of the data dots completely unobstructed, ensuring 100% scan reliability across both older iPhone and Android models.

Step 4: Set Error Correction to Level Q or H

Error correction compensates for the area covered by your logo. Selecting Level Q or Level H ensures that your smartphone camera easily reads the code even in low-light environments.

Real-World Sizing Rule of Thumb: Maintain a 10:1 scanning ratio. For every 10 inches (or centimeters) of distance between the customer and the QR code, increase the barcode width by 1 inch (or centimeter). A menu scanned from across a table (20 inches away) should be at least 2 x 2 inches wide.
Field-Tested Note: We always recommend running the 'Two-Phone Daylight Test' before sending any QR code to a commercial printer. Test scan the exported vector on both an iPhone and an older Android phone—first under direct bright sunlight, and then in a dimly lit hallway. If both open in under one second, your design is print-ready.
