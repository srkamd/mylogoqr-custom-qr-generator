Why Do QR Codes Expire? The Subscription Trap & Permanent Fix

By Shahrukh Ahmad • Published November 15, 2026 • 8 min read

Every week, thousands of small business owners, wedding couples, and graphic designers experience the same nightmare: they print hundreds of brochures, restaurant table tents, product packaging boxes, or banners, only to discover a few weeks later that their QR code has suddenly expired.

When someone scans the code, instead of seeing their website or menu, a terrifying paywall appears: "This campaign has expired. Please upgrade to a Pro plan ($15-$45/month) to reactivate your code."

The Dirty Secret of "Dynamic" QR Code Vendors

Standard QR codes created under the global ISO/IEC 18004 standard do not possess an expiration timer. A QR code is nothing more than optical data—black and white modules arranged to represent binary bits. Just like printed text on paper doesn't vanish, a real QR code cannot expire.

So why do so many online generators expire? Because they trick you into creating a "Dynamic" redirect link.

How the "Free Trial" Hostage Model Works:

  1. You enter your website mycafe.com/menu on a seemingly free generator.
  2. The vendor does not encode mycafe.com/menu into the QR image.
  3. Instead, they encode their own server link: qrcodegenerator.com/r/84920x.
  4. For the first 14 days, their server redirects visitors to your menu.
  5. On day 15, their database turns off the forwarder, forcing you to pay recurring monthly subscription fees forever.
