QR Code with “Scan Me” Text: How to Add It + Best Practices

By Shahrukh Ahmad — Creator & Lead Systems Architect  |  Last updated: November 2026

Quick Answer

Place “Scan Me” text below the QR code, outside the quiet zone, in a high-contrast color. Font size: at least 8pt printed / 14px digital. Use action-specific text (“Scan to Order”, “Scan for Menu”) for even better results.

Why “Scan Me” Text Matters

Not everyone instinctively knows to scan a QR code — especially older audiences or those seeing a QR code for the first time in a new context (a flyer, a product package, a table card). A clear call-to-action label removes that hesitation.

The text serves two purposes:

  • Instruction: tells people what to do (scan it)
  • Expectation: tells people what will happen (see the menu, connect to Wi-Fi, get a discount)

Placement Rules

The QR standard (ISO/IEC 18004) requires a quiet zone — a blank white border around the code. Your text must go outside this quiet zone. Never overlay text on the QR modules themselves.

✓ Correct: Text Below the QR Code

Most common. The quiet zone acts as natural spacing. Leave at least 8px gap between the bottom of the quiet zone and the top of your text.

✓ Correct: Text Inside a Frame Around the QR Code

A colored frame with the text in a banner strip (bottom or top). The frame is decorative and outside the QR data area. This is the most visually professional approach.

✗ Wrong: Text Overlapping the QR Modules

Any text that covers QR data modules will cause scan failures. Even with Level H error correction, text overlay destroys too many modules in a clustered area.

Font Size & Readability Guidelines

Context QR Size Min. Text Size
Business card / flyer 2.5 × 2.5 cm 6pt / 8px
Restaurant table card / poster 5 × 5 cm 10pt / 14px
Retail shelf / display 8 × 8 cm 14pt / 18px
Digital screen / website 150 × 150 px+ 14px

Color & Contrast for the Text

The “Scan Me” text should contrast strongly against its background. WCAG 2.1 recommends at least 4.5:1 contrast ratio for text. For print, use black text on white, or white text on a dark-colored frame banner.

  • ✓ Black text on white background: 21:1 ratio (perfect)
  • ✓ White text on brand-colored frame: check with Contrast Checker
  • ✗ Gray text on white: often fails (check carefully)
  • ✗ Light text on light background: always fails

Frame Designs That Work

A frame turns a plain QR code into a branded, action-oriented design. Here are the most effective frame patterns:

□

Simple Border + Bottom Banner

A thin colored border around the QR with a colored strip at the bottom containing “Scan Me” in white. Clean, professional, works at any size.

📷

Speech Bubble Frame

QR code inside a rounded rectangle with the text in a small tab above or below. Great for social posts and event signage.

🚩

Top Label + Bottom CTA

Brand name or headline at top, QR in middle, action text at bottom. Best for retail and packaging where you want brand reinforcement and clear instruction together.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does "Scan Me" text improve scan rates?

Yes. Explicit call-to-action labels improve engagement, especially among audiences less familiar with QR codes. Action-specific text (“Scan for Menu”) outperforms generic “Scan Me” because it sets expectations.

Where should the text be placed?

Below the QR code, outside the quiet zone (white border). Leave at least 8px gap. Never overlay text on the QR modules.

What font size should I use?

Minimum 8pt for print (business card scale), 14px for digital. The text should be legible from the same viewing distance as the QR code itself.

Can I use custom text instead of "Scan Me"?

Absolutely. “Scan to Order”, “Scan for Menu”, “Scan to Connect”, “Scan to Pay” — all work better than generic text because they tell the user what to expect.
