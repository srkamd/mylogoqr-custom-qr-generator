QR Code Technical Glossary & ISO/IEC Standards

Written by Shahrukh Ahmad — Technical Systems Guide • Last updated October 2026

Core Standards

  • ISO/IEC 18004: The international standard defining two-dimensional QR code matrix symbology.
  • Finder Patterns: The three distinct concentric square targets located at the top-left, top-right, and bottom-left corners.
  • Timing Pattern: Alternating dark and light modules connecting finder patterns to calibrate coordinate grid scale.
  • Data Masking: Algorithmic XOR patterns applied to prevent large blocks of identical color modules.

Essential QR Code Terminology

Finder Patterns (Position Detection Patterns)
The three large square targets in three corners of the matrix. They allow cameras to identify the QR code angle, orientation, and perspective distortion instantaneously at 360 degrees.
Alignment Pattern
Smaller concentric square patterns present in Version 2 and higher codes. They help the scanner software correct for surface curvature on bottles, cans, or bent paper.
Quiet Zone
A blank boundary buffer surrounding the entire code, standardized at a minimum width of 4 modules, essential for camera border segmentation.
Modules
The individual black and white square units that make up the two-dimensional matrix grid.
Reed-Solomon Parity Blocks
Mathematical polynomials appended to the raw payload data that enable automatic data recovery when parts of the code are obscured by dirt, scratches, or custom logos.
