How to Print QR Codes: Sizing, DPI & Print Best Practices

Written by Shahrukh Ahmad — Technical Print Guide • Last updated November 2026
style="max-width: 900px; margin: 0 auto; padding: 20px; line-height: 1.8; color: #334155;">

Printing a QR code incorrectly is one of the most expensive marketing mistakes a business can make. If a printed code fails to scan on brochures, product boxes, or billboards, reprinting costs thousands of dollars. Follow this comprehensive pre-flight printing guide to guarantee 100% optical readability.

1. Minimum Print Dimensions by Media Type

The standard rule of thumb for QR code sizing is the 10:1 distance-to-size ratio. For example, if a user stands 10 inches away, the QR code should measure at least 1 inch wide. If scanning from 10 feet away (such as a trade show backdrop), the code must measure at least 12 inches (1 foot) wide.

Print Medium Typical Scan Distance Recommended Minimum Size
Visiting & Business Cards 6 - 10 inches (15 - 25 cm) 0.8" x 0.8" (20 x 20 mm)
Table Tents & Restaurant Menus 12 - 18 inches (30 - 45 cm) 1.2" x 1.2" (30 x 30 mm)
Flyers, Brochures & Catalogs 1 - 2 feet (30 - 60 cm) 1.5" x 1.5" (38 x 38 mm)
Window Decals & Posters 3 - 6 feet (1 - 2 meters) 4.0" x 4.0" (100 x 100 mm)
Trade Show Banners & Backdrops 8 - 15 feet (2.5 - 5 meters) 12" x 12" (300 x 300 mm)+

2. Resolution: Why Vector SVG & 300 DPI PNG are Mandatory

Web graphics typically render at 72 DPI (dots per inch), which looks blurry and jagged when printed on physical offset or digital commercial presses. For razor-sharp module edges:

  • Vector SVG or EPS: Always provide your graphic designer or print shop with vector SVG. Vector files have infinite resolution and scale to billboard size with zero pixelation or blur.
  • Raster PNG (300 DPI+): If using raster images, download the 2000x2000 pixel export from MyLogoQR. When placed at a 2-inch print width, a 2000px image delivers an astonishing 1000 DPI, far surpassing standard print requirements.

3. The Quiet Zone: Why White Margins Matter

A QR code reader identifies the start and end of the data matrix by finding the empty margin surrounding the code, known as the Quiet Zone. The quiet zone must be at least 4 modules wide on all four sides. Never bleed text, background illustrations, or edge borders directly into the code modules, as this will prevent camera autofocus from locking on.

4. Material Finishes: Matte vs Glossy Substrates

Camera glare from harsh overhead fluorescent lights or outdoor sunlight can blind optical sensors. Whenever possible, print QR codes on matte or satin finishes rather than ultra-glossy or metallic foil laminates. If using clear acrylic table stands, angle them slightly back to disperse ambient lighting glare.

Pre-Print Scannability Checklist

  • ✓ Exported in Vector SVG or 300 DPI high-definition PNG
  • ✓ Dark foreground modules on a light/white background (high contrast)
  • ✓ Quiet zone padding intact around all 4 edges
  • ✓ Tested under dim indoor lighting and bright daylight on both iOS and Android cameras
  • ✓ Center logo does not obstruct the 3 corner position finder squares
